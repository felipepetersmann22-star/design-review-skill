# Review lenses

Use the applicable lenses as perspectives on the user's real task. A label alone is not a finding. Tie each observation to a visible element or an exercised interaction and explain its effect.

## Purpose and content

Can the intended person understand where they are, what matters, what action comes next and what will happen? Use real, representative content and long values. Inspect reading order, labels, comparison, truthfulness of claims and data, and whether repeated summaries compete with the working area. Do not invent testimonials, statistics, live status or product capabilities. Mark examples as examples.

## Composition and identity

Look at focal point, information density, shared edges, grouping, whitespace as a relationship, alignment, line length, type roles and wrapping. Examine color's semantic role, contrast, imagery, icons, shape and surfaces. Ask whether the visual language is coherent and specific to this product and audience, including where familiar conventions support the task. Preserve strong existing character. Choose the overall composition before polishing individual controls.

## Anti-slop diagnosis

Investigate unchosen defaults: repetitive equally weighted blocks with no information hierarchy, decorative labels or containers that add no meaning, generic claims, arbitrary stock imagery, overemphasized metrics, disconnected visual styles, and surface polish covering dead or implausible interactions. Explain the particular repetition or mismatch and its consequence, then propose a task-specific repair, such as regrouping content or replacing vague copy with a supported fact.

No visual treatment is inherently slop. Gradients, glass, dark themes, large type, rounded cards, bento grids, icons, system fonts and standard components can work when they serve the content and audience. Equal cards may be appropriate for comparable choices. A distinctive design need not invent new controls or reject a library. Judge purpose, coherence, readability and behavior, not the mere presence of a style.

## Interaction and continuity

For each relevant action trace trigger → feedback → commit → cancel or reverse → recovery. Distinguish focus, selection, pending, disabled, error and success. Look for dead controls, duplicate submission, lost input, misleading progress and unclear consequences. Test exits and return focus for dialogs; offer visible or keyboard alternatives for gestures. Respect browser navigation, text selection and scrolling. Define whether drafts and choices survive Back, dismissal and reload; test each separately. Explicit Save and autosave need different, clearly communicated commitments. Inspection alone must not mutate data.

## Accessibility and responsive behavior

Inspect semantic controls and labels, logical keyboard order, visible focus, error association, readable contrast, non-color status cues, enlarged text, target usability and reduced motion. For forms, maintain persistent and programmatically associated labels, understandable requirements, recoverable values and useful error messages. Choose validation timing and form structure for the task. Verify standards and platform requirements from current authoritative sources if making compliance claims.

Check narrow, intermediate and wide layouts with realistic and long content, and short heights where relevant. Reflow prose, forms and navigation; prevent accidental page overflow, clipped controls and hidden focus. A deliberately bounded, keyboard-reachable horizontal comparison region can be appropriate for genuinely two-dimensional data. Simulated resizing is not a physical-device or native zoom test.
